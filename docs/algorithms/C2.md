# C2. Availability analysis

Increment: I4b (M5, 5 November 2026). Both levels are finished.

## Two levels

- **Level 1 (known algorithm, our transformation):** Guarded dataflow fixpoint: a fact is a value plus the condition under which it exists; transfer functions per join rule.
- **Level 2 (built on the output of level 1):** Path pruning: facts on paths that C6 proves infeasible are dropped, so 'maybe unavailable' warnings disappear where the branches exclude each other.
- **Dependence:** Level 2 calls the solver of C6 on the conditions collected by level 1.
- **Baseline for the benchmark:** Plain availability analysis (no conditions), against ours: false warnings on the reference corpus.

## Problem

When a step reads the output of another (`${approve.decision}`, `${check[*].rows}`), that output
exists only if the other step ran and succeeded. In a workflow with guards and four join rules
this is not obvious from the graph: `approve` may be skipped, a `continue` step may have failed,
a quorum may have been met by fewer instances than the reader indexes. A reference to an output
that does not exist stops the run at that step. The designer should be told when saving, with the
condition that breaks it and a value of the inputs that shows it, and should not be told when the
read is safe because the guards exclude the failing case.

## Why a library does not solve it

Definite-assignment analyses in compilers (Java's, Kotlin's) work on straight-line code and
structured branches; they have no notion of a join that waits for a quorum or of a branch that is
guarded by a condition on a decision made by a person at run time. Graph libraries give
dominators and reachability, which answer "is X an ancestor on every path", the plain baseline
below. The question here is "if Y starts, has X succeeded", which depends on the conditions on
the paths and so needs a solver; ours is `solver/` (C6), because a SAT/SMT library is not allowed.

## What is borrowed (textbook algorithms, named)

- Forward must-analysis on a DAG in topological order, one pass (the "available expressions" shape of Kildall, 1973): a fact survives a join only when every incoming path has it.
- Facts guarded by a path condition (as in path-sensitive and conditional dataflow analyses): a fact is a formula, not a bit.

## What is ours (the transformation and the code we write)

`analysis/runs.py` (about 120 lines) and `analysis/availability.py` (about 450 lines), spec 10.

- `ran(Y)`: the exact condition under which Y runs in an execution where nothing fails, built from the guard and the join rule of Y over the conditions of its dependencies (a quorum is the disjunction of the conjunctions of k of them, capped at 64 combinations, and the whole condition at 200 nodes). `bounds` gives the two predicates that enclose it when an atom is outside the fragment of the solver: opaque as false (a lower bound) and as true (an upper bound).
- `Av(Y, X)`: a sufficient condition for "if Y starts, X has succeeded". `all_success`: the disjunction over dependencies of "X is that dependency, or Av of it". `any_success` and `at_least`: the conjunction over dependencies of "it did not run, or it passes the fact on", with the upper bound of its run condition. `all_done` passes nothing on. A step with no `on_failure: continue` at or above it succeeded exactly when it ran, so its own lower-bound run condition is also a fact.
- A reference in `with` or `ensures` is valid when the guard of the reader entails `Av`; in `when` the assumption is `true`, and an `exists(x.f)` conjunct to the left proves the part to its right. The failing case comes back as a value of the inputs (a witness) and as the condition that skips the step.
- Group sizes (how many instances of a foreach step have surely succeeded, from the join rules), goals (`uncovered` over the run conditions for `any_of`, each goal for `all_of`), steps that are dead by inheritance (the run condition is unsatisfiable because an ancestor is dead), effects without `compensate`.

## Adaptation: where the plain textbook version is wrong

- Plain reachability (mandatory ancestors, the intersection of sets) gives a false warning when two branches with complementary guards both lead from a step that may fail with `continue` into an `any_success` join, because neither branch alone guarantees it and the intersection is empty. Test: `test_complementary_branches_make_a_common_ancestor_available` (no warning); `test_overlapping_branches_do_not` (warning, with a value of `amount`). In the measurement, plain reachability gives 278 false warnings on 648 safe pairs, ours 55.
- A rule stated with the guards of the dependencies instead of their run conditions (the first reading of the Proposal's formula) cannot see that a guard is skipped because an ancestor was; the readiness audit replaced the guard by `ran`. The oracle test is what shows the replacement sound.
- The audit also claimed that a failed `continue` predecessor makes the `any_success` formula unsound. Re-deriving the rule for the implementation showed it does not (the failed step is not a success, so it does not decide the join), and the claim and the special case were removed from the code, the spec and the milestone text. The journal records it.
- An opaque atom (`len(...) > 2`) must not turn into a claim either way: read as false when a fact is built and as true when a run condition is negated, so what the analysis cannot read costs a fact and never produces a false statement. When the solver cannot decide, the note I-AVAIL-003 says so and no warning is made.

## Pseudocode

```text
runs[Y]   = guard(Y) and gate(join(Y), [runs[P] for P in needs(Y)])      # topological order
for Y in plan order:
    facts = {}
    for X in names mentioned by the dependencies' facts, or the dependencies themselves:
        per P in needs(Y): known[P][X] = TRUE if X is P else Av[P][X]
        all_success:           facts[X] = OR_P known[P][X]
        any_success/at_least:  facts[X] = AND_P ( not hi(runs[P]) or known[P][X] )
        all_done:              facts[X] = nothing
    for X above Y that is clean (no continue at or above X):  facts[X] |= lo(runs[X])
    drop facts of more than 300 nodes
reference to X in Y valid  iff  entails(guard(Y), Av[Y][X])            # UNSAT of guard and not Av
```

## Complexity

One predicate per (step, ancestor) pair, each at most 300 nodes: O(n^2) predicates for n steps,
each built in time linear in its size, and one entailment query per reference (the solver's
cost is in `docs/algorithms/C6.md`: DNF capped at 64 clauses). Measured: 0.4 ms for the example
workflow of 22 steps, 4 ms on average for 50 random steps and 42 ms (at most 102 ms) for 200.

## Oracle (independent check)

`tests/unit/analysis/test_availability.py`, against the reference interpreter in `tests/reference/`
(own join table, own evaluator, no import of the engine or of the analysis):

- Soundness: for random workflows with `continue` steps, random input values and random sets of
  failing steps (4 seeds x 150 workflows x 8 runs), whenever a step starts and its fact `Av(Y, X)`
  holds, X has succeeded in that execution. In the first seed 1,377 facts were checked, 242 of them
  not trivially true (the test requires more than 1,000 and 200 per seed, so it cannot pass empty).
- Exactness: with no failure, a step runs exactly when its run condition holds.
- Two deliberate faults fail the tests (`not low` in place of `not high` for the quorum rule;
  dropping the fact that a clean step succeeded iff it ran).

## Measurement

`benchmarks/bench_availability.py`, `docs/research/availability-measurement.md`: 250 random
workflows, 648 safe pairs. Plain reachability recognises 57.1% and warns falsely on 278; ours
recognises 91.5% and warns falsely on 55 (80.2% fewer). Neither analysis lets an unsafe pair
through.

## Stated limits

- The analysis is a sufficient condition: some safe reads still get a warning (8.5% of the safe pairs in the measurement; mostly a step that needs two dependencies of which one depends on the other, behind `at_least` or `any_success`). A warning never means "will fail", it means "not shown to be safe".
- Failures are modelled for steps with `on_failure: continue` only (a failure of any other step stops the run, spec 5.4). Retries and compensation do not change what a started step can read.
- Conditions on values the solver does not read (a call, a string function, a non-linear expression) are opaque; a read that depends on them gets the note I-AVAIL-003 and no judgement.
- A predicate over 300 nodes and a quorum over more than 64 combinations lose the fact (the warning appears; the analysis stays sound).
- A run condition over 200 nodes (`RUN_CAP`) is given up and becomes an opaque atom, which `bounds` reads as "may or may not run". The condition of a step holds the conditions of the steps it needs, so in a chain of guarded steps it grows by two nodes per step (one more guard and one more conjunction) and nests as deep as the chain is long; without the cap the analysis ran out of stack at about 500 steps (US-43). A chain is exact up to about 100 guarded steps in a row (fewer when a guard has several atoms) and, past that, the steps after it lose their facts (a warning may appear, never a missing one). The size is counted without recursion (`runs.within`). The time of the analysis is still above linear in the length of the chain (1.9 s for 1,000 steps, 5.8 s for 2,000; `research/deep-chains.md`).
- The unit of a fact is a step output as a whole; a field that is missing from an output that exists is the business of the type check (E-TYPE-002), and the existence of a list element in a group is the counts of dsl-spec 10.3 (guaranteed number of instances), not the data in it.
